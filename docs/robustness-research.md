# RGB-only robustness: research and next experiments

23 September 2026. This is a design review, not a claim that the proposed models work in this simulator or on an SO-100. The frozen policy's [stress report](../eval/ood-v1/REPORT.md) found 16/28 successes: unseen colors misgrounded, novel nouns sometimes truncated the skill sequence, a teal table became a false object, and correct targets sometimes had bad grasp points.

## Best near-term change

1. **Propose objects independently of their color or the expected count.** With a fixed camera, capture a *real RGB frame of the empty tabletop* at setup and find changed foreground regions in the live RGB image. Keep the arm at the same home pose for both frames and exclude the known arm workspace or compare against its home image. This is a generic, class-agnostic foreground method, not a rule for purple cubes or teal tables. OpenCV documents foreground segmentation against a background model for static cameras. It needs a genuinely empty-table calibration frame; generating one from simulator object poses during policy inference would leak privileged state. Shadows, camera movement, lighting changes, identical table/object colors, and touching objects can still break the masks. [OpenCV background subtraction](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d1/dc5/tutorial_background_subtraction.html).
2. **Let the pretrained vision-language model rank those object regions.** Compare the existing trained target head with zero-shot image/text similarity on each masked crop. Neither a fixed four-cluster count nor the head's high confidence should be treated as object evidence: our own teal-table and pink-block trials disprove that. This is an *experiment*, not an established improvement. Score every candidate and retain the runner-up margin; when candidates are indistinguishable, record ambiguity instead of presenting confidence as certainty. Open-vocabulary text-conditioned localization has stronger published alternatives: [Grounding DINO](https://github.com/IDEA-Research/GroundingDINO) detects boxes from category names or referring expressions; [OWLv2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09683) detects text-query boxes and has an [official Transformers path](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/owlv2). Boxes are not grasp masks.
3. **Correct the final approach with a new RGB frame.** After IK reaches a safe height over the selected region, compare the visible gripper tip and target in the image, make a small correction, observe again, and only then lower. Monocular visual servoing has demonstrated correction of reach errors and novel-object grasping, though the cited learned system used over 800,000 training grasps and does **not** provide an off-the-shelf SO-100 policy. The calibrated image-error loop is the transferable idea; the present calibration and gripper geometry still require bench measurement. [Levine et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02199); [image-based final approach with RGB feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.05650) (that system used RGB-D earlier in its pipeline).
4. **Separate language meaning from physical skill execution.** A pretrained local language model can select or plan from the existing ten skills, given their definitions and the current arm state, with no training on the failure phrases. Re-observe after each physical skill and replan after a failed grasp. This follows [SayCan's](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01691) language-usefulness × skill-feasibility split. For an Apple-Silicon experiment, [MLX LM](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm) runs local text models; a small [Qwen3 checkpoint](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3) is a candidate, **not a validated planner here**. Constraining output to the ten physical skills limits hallucinated motor commands, but those ten skills cannot accomplish every free-text request. The planner must be tested on withheld paraphrases such as “approach,” “lift,” “move then release,” and nonsense commands without adding phrase-specific branches.

## Model alternatives worth a measured ablation

| Option | What it gives | Practical caveat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [CLIPSeg](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Luddecke_Image_Segmentation_Using_Text_and_Image_Prompts_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf) | A text-conditioned pixel map from one RGB image. | Does not inherently separate multiple same-description instances; test on the actual MuJoCo renders before integration. |
| [OWLv2](https://huggingface.co/google/owlv2-base-patch16-ensemble) or [Grounding DINO](https://github.com/IDEA-Research/GroundingDINO) + [SAM 2.1 tiny](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2) | Text-conditioned boxes followed by prompted, object-shaped masks; SAM 2 can also track masks through video. | Additional models and latency. OWLv2 base weights are about 620 MB; SAM 2.1 tiny has 38.9M parameters. SAM 2's published speed is on an A100, **not** this Mac. A box/mask can still be wrong on synthetic imagery. |
| [Depth Anything V2 small](https://github.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2) | A pretrained *relative* depth cue from RGB; official code includes an MPS device path. | Its relative-depth checkpoint is not metric depth. Separate indoor metric checkpoints exist, but neither establishes millimetre tabletop grasp accuracy here. Test against known simulated geometry before using it for Z. |
| [MonoGraspNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13036) | Research proof that RGB-only 6-DoF grasp prediction on unfamiliar objects is possible. | A trained grasp network, camera/domain transfer, runtime, and SO-100 gripper fit still need verification. It does not make an arbitrary single RGB view geometrically unambiguous. |

The most direct route on this M1 Max is **foreground proposals + the already loaded vision-language encoder + closed-loop RGB approach**. Try the heavier open-vocabulary models only if a held-out A/B test shows the simpler route misses too many real object regions. This prioritization is an engineering inference from the cited methods and this repo's failures, not a published performance ranking.

## Geometry boundary and honest test

A calibrated table plane lets an image point map to a **point on that plane**. It does not reveal the height, far side, or graspable face of an arbitrary object above the plane; the current fixed 30 mm top-height assumption is therefore a material limit. [OpenCV's planar homography explanation](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d9/dab/tutorial_homography.html) covers the plane case. A side RGB view plus the known arm geometry can supply *new observations* while approaching, but a single still frame cannot make occluded geometry visible. Relative monocular depth models do not remove the metric-scale problem by themselves. [Depth Anything V2 model notes](https://github.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2).

Keep all checkpoints frozen. First grade object masks, target identity, estimated grasp point, full skill sequence, and physical lift separately on the existing 28 trials and fresh seeds. Include camera shift, colored tables, compressed RGB, same-color objects, tall/short objects, transparent/glossy objects, and shapes whose contact differs from cubes. Record actual MuJoCo rollouts, not scripted arm trajectories. If a proposed method helps only the 28 inspected cases, report that and test new cases before making it the default.

## What this revision actually measured

The implemented policy follows the first two recommendations and uses frozen Qwen3-4B for one plan per command. It still lacks a calibrated final-approach visual-servo loop. The [complete rollout report](../eval/ood-v2/REPORT.md) is 21/28 on the already inspected scenes, 8/13 on the first new set, and 6/10 on the second. The remaining failures show that language planning and target selection alone do not solve contact geometry.

CLIPSeg was tried on eight actual simulator frames: it had weak target activation for the purple cube and blue tube, and strongly activated the teal tabletop for “green cylinder.” OWLv2 often returned broad arm/scene boxes at low scores for the same small rendered objects. A larger frozen SigLIP 2 model was also tested on the saved crop-ranking task; its full-instruction ranking was worse than the existing base model. These are local ablations, not published comparisons. The larger models were not integrated. The 1.5B language model generated invalid or incomplete skill lists; Qwen3-4B passed the five core end-to-end checks and was retained. No checkpoint was updated.

The next substantial unknown is the **grasp**, especially for flat, tall, rounded, or partly hidden objects. A second calibrated RGB view or an actively moved camera could reduce geometric ambiguity without a depth sensor. With one fixed side view, the current 30 mm plane assumption and gripper contact behavior remain the limiting factors; the cited monocular grasp research does not provide an immediately transferable SO-100 solution.
